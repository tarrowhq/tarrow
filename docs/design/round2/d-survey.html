D — The Survey

The question is geometric, and no version of this interface has ever drawn it. "Within 1,000 feet, measured parcel boundary to parcel boundary" is a statement about shapes on ground; answering it in paragraphs makes the reader rebuild the picture in their head while frightened.

This is a survey plat. The address is a parcel, the buffer is a ring, each school is a parcel, and every distance is a dimension line — annotated the way drawings have carried high-stakes information for two centuries. There is no body copy on the result screen. Every sentence is a label on the drawing or a numbered margin note.

Why no product does this, and why tarrow can: a normal web map fetches tiles from a third party, handing over the reader's IP and the area they are studying. This drawing fetches nothing — it is SVG built from parcel geometry the server already holds and already measured. It adds no request at all.

Note 1 — why a school appears twice

Ohio's rule is written about the premises — the parcel of land — not the building. A school owning several parcels appears once per parcel. A and B are the same school. Repeated names are not a mistake.

Note 2 — how these were measured

Nearest point of one parcel boundary to the nearest point of the other, never centre to centre. 318 of the 341 premises tarrow holds have a real boundary.

Note 3 — not drawn: facility classes

Preschools, licensed child day-care, children's crisis care and residential infant care are not on this sheet because they are not loaded at all. Their absence from the drawing is not their absence from the ground.

Note 4 — not drawn: municipal rules

No city or village ordinance is loaded. Summit County municipalities set their own rules, and none of them is on this sheet. Only the state's 1,000 ft school rule is drawn here.

Note 5 — unsigned

No person has checked the rule tarrow applied. tarrow read the statute and applied a number; nobody signed their name to that reading, and no court was asked whether this is how the state measures. A real plat is stamped by a surveyor. This one is not.

Note 6 — what tarrow kept

Nothing. The address is not in a log, a URL, a database, or a browser history entry. How to check that yourself.

Note 1 — what this sheet is not

An empty drawing is not "nothing nearby". tarrow does not know where this address is, so it measured no distances and found nothing — neither near nor far.

Note 2 — what to try

The address may not be in Summit County; the county may write it differently (road type, direction, unit); or the county may not have published a point for it. Try it as it appears on a tax bill or lease.

Note 3 — why tarrow left it blank

It will not fall back to the middle of the street or the centre of the ZIP code, and it will not offer the nearest parcel it happened to find. A confident drawing of the wrong building is more dangerous to you than a blank sheet.
