F — The Worksheet

There is no results page. There is a form that fills itself in. Every other direction — including D and E — accepts the premise "user submits, app responds with a page." This one rejects it.

tarrow's constitutional job is to prepare somebody for a phone call, so the artifact is the thing they carry into it: a worksheet in the first person, where tarrow fills the parts it can and leaves blank the parts only the sheriff can answer.

The blanks are the point. A blank is a physical hole in the reader's knowledge. It cannot be skimmed past the way a paragraph of qualification can, and it carries a caption naming who must fill it. Coverage stops being a disclosure section and becomes the literal unfinished half of the document.

  I asked about .......  [1464 GARMAN RD, AKRON]     <- the reader wrote this
  tarrow measured .....  [3 premises within 1000 ft] <- tarrow wrote this
  City or village rules  [__________________]        <- NOBODY has written this

It prints. Not a gimmick: the reader may be at a library terminal with no way to keep a tab open, and paper survives the browser closing.

Sheet 1 · start the worksheet

Let's fill in what we can.

tarrow measures one thing — distance to school premises in Summit County, Ohio. It fills in that line and leaves the rest for the sheriff's office.

tarrow does not correct spelling, and it does not guess. Type the address as it appears on a bill. If it cannot find exactly what you typed, it says so rather than answering about the wrong building.

e.g. 1464 Garman Rd, Akron, OH 44313

tarrow will not fall back to the middle of the street or the centre of the ZIP code, and will not offer the nearest parcel it happened to find. A confident answer about the wrong building is more dangerous than no answer. Recorded reason: address-not-matched.
