Direction C — Call Sheet

The constitution promises "an hour of searching plus one confirming phone call." This direction designs for the second half of that sentence. The page is built as the thing the reader carries into the call, and the answer is deliberately not the loudest element — because the answer is a measurement and the call is the action.

The signature and the risk are the same object: one reversed-ink card holding what the reader will say out loud, numbered because it is read in order down a phone. It is the only inverted block on the page, so it cannot be mistaken for a verdict. It appears on every shape, including the ones where tarrow failed.

Why this happens, and what to try

  • The address is not in Summit County, Ohio. tarrow holds no data for anywhere else, and it cannot tell an out-of-county address apart from a misspelled one, so both land on this page.
  • The county writes it differently. Try it as it appears on a tax bill, a utility bill, or a lease.
  • The county has not published a point for it. New addresses, and some municipalities generally, are thinly covered.

tarrow will not guess: it does not correct spelling, it does not fall back to the middle of the street or the centre of the ZIP code, and it does not offer the nearest parcel it happened to find instead. A confident answer about the wrong building is more dangerous to you than no answer at all.
